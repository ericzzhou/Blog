What is Ramune?

A 150-year-old soda with a marble in the bottle — here's how it became Japan's most iconic drink.

Ramune (ラムネ) is a Japanese carbonated soft drink famous for its unique Codd-neck bottle, sealed with a glass marble. The name comes from the English word "lemonade," and the drink has been a beloved part of Japanese culture since the 1870s. It is the quintessential Japanese soda — as iconic in Japan as cola is in America.

Today, Ramune is synonymous with Japanese summer festivals (matsuri). Every year, millions of bottles are sold during fireworks displays, temple festivals, and Obon celebrations. The satisfying pop of the marble dropping into the bottle is the soundtrack of Japanese summers. Vendors at festival stalls (yatai) sell ice-cold Ramune alongside takoyaki, yakisoba, and cotton candy — it's an experience that defines the season.

Beyond festivals, Ramune has become a global phenomenon through anime and manga. Characters are frequently shown drinking Ramune at summer festival scenes, and fans worldwide have grown curious about this mysterious marble-sealed bottle. In recent years, Ramune has also become a popular gift item and party drink in the US, appreciated for its unique opening ritual and clean, refreshing taste.

1870s: English Origins

Hiram Codd invented the Codd-neck bottle in England in 1872 to seal carbonated drinks. The marble-and-gasket design was revolutionary — no caps or corks needed. British bottlers quickly adopted it for mineral water and lemonade.

🇯🇵

1884: Arrives in Japan

Scottish pharmacist Alexander Cameron Sim introduced the drink to Japan. Japanese consumers loved the fizzy lemonade and the playful marble bottle. The word "lemonade" was adapted to "Ramune" (ラムネ) in katakana.

🎆

Today: Festival Icon

Ramune is now inseparable from Japanese matsuri culture. Multiple brands produce dozens of flavors. It's exported worldwide and has become a staple of anime, manga, and Japanese pop culture globally.

The Science of the Marble Bottle

How does a glass marble keep soda fizzy for months? It's brilliant physics.

1

The Codd-Neck Design

The bottle neck has a narrow chamber with a rubber gasket ring. A glass marble sits above the gasket. When the bottle is filled with carbonated liquid, the internal CO2 pressure pushes the marble upward, pressing it firmly against the gasket to create an airtight seal.

2

Why the Marble Stays in Place

The carbonation pressure (typically 3-4 volumes of CO2) exerts constant upward force on the marble. Gravity and the pressure work together — the marble cannot fall because the gas pushes it up, and it cannot escape because the neck is narrower than the marble. The result is a self-sealing bottle that needs no cap.

3

The Lower Chamber

Below the narrow neck, the bottle widens into a larger chamber. When you push the marble down, it drops into this chamber and rattles around while you drink. The narrow neck prevents the marble from blocking the opening or falling out. Two grooves on either side of the neck allow liquid to flow past the marble when pouring.

4

The Physics of Carbonation

Ramune is carbonated to roughly 3.5 volumes of CO2 — similar to champagne but less aggressive than beer. This gives it fine, delicate bubbles that feel silky on the palate rather than harsh. The marble seal maintains this pressure consistently until you open it.

How to Open a Ramune Bottle

First time? Don't worry — it's easier than it looks. Follow these steps and you'll be fizzing in seconds.

1

Remove the Seal

Peel off the plastic shrink wrap around the bottle cap. Underneath, you'll see a plastic flipper cap and the marble visible through the glass neck. The flipper is the plunger you'll use to push the marble down.

2

Position the Plunger

Place the bottle on a flat surface. The plastic flipper cap is already hinged at the top — flip it up so it sits above the marble. Some brands include a separate plunger; if so, place it on top of the marble.

3

Push Down Firmly

Using both thumbs, press down firmly on the plunger (or flipper). You'll feel resistance from the carbonation pressure, then a satisfying POP as the marble drops into the lower chamber. Hold for 2-3 seconds.

4

Wait for the Fizz

After the marble drops, the soda will fizz vigorously for a few seconds — like opening a shaken champagne bottle. Wait until the fizzing subsides before removing the plunger and drinking. This prevents overflow.

5

Remove the Plunger & Drink

Once the fizzing has calmed, lift off the plunger. The marble will rattle freely in the lower chamber. Drink directly from the bottle — the marble stays in the bottom and won't block your mouth thanks to the neck grooves.

⚡

Pro Tips

Chill the bottle before opening — cold soda holds carbonation better and fizz is more controlled. Never shake it first. Point the bottle away from others when pushing the marble down, just in case of a fizzy surprise.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you wanted to know about Ramune

What does Ramune taste like?
Original Ramune tastes like a light, mildly sweet lemon-lime soda with exceptionally fine, delicate carbonation. The flavor is often described as a cross between Sprite and cream soda, but lighter and less syrupy. Flavored varieties taste like their respective fruits — strawberry, melon, grape, and lychee are among the most popular. The carbonation level is gentler than Western sodas, giving it a smooth, refined mouthfeel.
How do you open a Ramune bottle?
Remove the plastic seal and flipper from the bottle top. Place the bottle on a flat surface. Using both thumbs, push down firmly on the plastic plunger until the marble drops into the lower chamber with a satisfying pop. Hold for 2-3 seconds as the fizz subsides, then remove the plunger. The marble will rattle around in the lower chamber while you drink — this is completely normal. For best results, chill the bottle first and never shake it before opening.
Is Ramune alcoholic?
No, Ramune is completely non-alcoholic. It is a carbonated soft drink (soda) made from carbonated water, sugar, and fruit flavoring. It is safe for children and is one of the most popular drinks at Japanese summer festivals for all ages. The carbonation comes from dissolved CO2, not fermentation.
Where can I buy Ramune in the US?
You can buy Ramune at Asian grocery stores like H-Mart and Mitsuwa, online at Yami (yami.com), Weee!, and Amazon. Many mainstream Walmart and Target stores also carry Sangaria brand Ramune in their international food aisle. Prices typically range from $2.49 to $4.99 per bottle, with multi-packs offering better per-unit value.
What is the marble in Ramune for?
The marble is the sealing mechanism. Ramune uses a Codd-neck bottle design invented by Hiram Codd in 1872, where a glass marble sits in the neck of the bottle. The carbonation pressure pushes the marble up against a rubber gasket, creating an airtight seal that keeps the drink fizzy for months without a cap. To open, you push the marble down into a wider chamber below the neck. The marble stays there while you drink — two grooves on the sides of the neck allow liquid to flow past it.
