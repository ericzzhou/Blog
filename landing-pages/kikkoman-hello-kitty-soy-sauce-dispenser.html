A Design Icon, Reimagined

The Kikkoman soy sauce dispenser is in the Museum of Modern Art's permanent collection. Now it wears Hello Kitty's face — and it's the most adorable MoMA piece you'll ever own.

1961
The Original Dispenser

Industrial designer Kenji Ekuan creates the glass soy sauce dispenser for Kikkoman — a design so revolutionary it would later earn a spot in the Museum of Modern Art's permanent collection in New York. The drip-free spout poured with precision and grace. No more sticky bottles, no more stained tablecloths. It changed how the world serves soy sauce, and its elegant silhouette became instantly recognizable in restaurants from Tokyo to New York.

MoMA
Permanent Collection

The Museum of Modern Art in New York adds the Kikkoman dispenser to its permanent collection, recognizing it as a masterpiece of industrial design. It sits alongside the Eames chair and the Apple Macintosh — proof that everyday objects can achieve extraordinary beauty.

1974 → Today
Hello Kitty's Global Journey

Since her debut in 1974, Hello Kitty has become one of the world's most licensed characters, with merchandise sold in over 140 countries and regions. She even traveled to space — a Hello Kitty doll was aboard the Japanese microsatellite Hodoyoshi3, launched in 2014. When Sanrio partners with Kikkoman, it's not just a product — it's a collision of two cultural forces that have shaped how the world sees Japanese design.
