Japanese Drinks in the US: What to Buy First

If you already live in the US, most online "must drink in Japan" lists are written for tourists in Tokyo convenience stores—not for someone staring at an H-Mart shelf or a Yami / Weee! combo pack. My rule: buy 5 bottles first, not 20. This guide walks through four real scenarios (first flight, Ramune mishaps, canned coffee, where to shop) and links to deeper guides on this site.

Chapter 1

Your First 5 Bottles (Tasting Flight)

Start with one bottle from each category so you learn what your household actually likes:

1. Ramune (original or melon) — half the fun is the marble bottle ritual.
2. Pocari Sweat — light electrolyte drink, great with spicy or salty food.
3. Japanese canned coffee (BOSS or Georgia black, unsweetened) — chill it first.
4. Yakult — one small bottle is enough to test the sweet probiotic vibe.
5. Bottled matcha latte — lower commitment than buying matcha powder and tools.

Skip giant single-flavor Ramune cases until you know your sweetness tolerance. Limited-edition sodas are fun—but easy regret purchases.

Tip: Combo packs on Yami or Weee! beat buying one random bottle on Amazon when you do not have a local Asian market.

Chapter 2

Ramune: Open It Without Wearing the Soda

Ramune sprays because people open it warm or push the plunger at an angle. Chill 2+ hours, lay the bottle flat, press the cap plunger straight down, wait 3 seconds after the pop, then drink.

Flavor-wise: original is citrus-clean; melon is candy-sweet. If you hate sugary sodas, start with original—not melon by the case.

Know this: Ramune is as much about the bottle experience as the taste—treat it as a fun trial, not a daily driver.

Chapter 3

Japanese Canned Coffee: Not Starbucks

BOSS and Georgia black cans taste like Japanese convenience-store coffee—smooth, lightly sweet or unsweetened, meant to be ice cold. Sweet milk coffee versions can feel like liquid candy if you are not used to Vietnamese-style sweetness.

Buy a single can at H-Mart or Mitsuwa before committing to a multi-can pack online.

Tip: Canned coffee pairs better with onigiri or a quick breakfast than with dessert—different use case from matcha lattes.

Chapter 4

Where to Buy in the US

H-Mart / Mitsuwa — best for single-bottle experiments when you have a store nearby.
Yami / Weee! — better for combo packs when you do not have an Asian supermarket.
Amazon — fine for sealed variety packs; watch shipping cost and expiry dates on drinks.

No Asian market nearby? Start with a 5–7 flavor combo online rather than a 24-count single flavor.

Tip: Match your channel to intent—single bottles for curiosity, combo packs for a weekend tasting party.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Japanese drink should I buy first in the US?

Buy one Ramune (original or melon), one Pocari Sweat, one unsweetened BOSS or Georgia canned coffee, one Yakult, and one bottled matcha latte. That five-bottle flight covers sodas, wellness drinks, coffee, and tea without committing to a full case.

Is Ramune worth it if I only care about taste?

Ramune is mid on pure flavor for many people—melon can taste like liquid candy. Buy it for the marble-bottle experience first. If you want cleaner citrus soda, try original; if you want melon flavor without the bottle ritual, Fanta Melon is an easier entry.

Where can I buy Japanese drinks online in the US?

Yami and Weee! ship variety packs nationwide. H-Mart and Mitsuwa are best for single bottles if you have a store nearby. Amazon works for sealed combo packs—double-check seller ratings and expiration dates on beverages.

Are Japanese canned coffees sweet?

BOSS and Georgia both sell unsweetened black cans and sweeter milk-coffee versions. For a first test, grab unsweetened black, refrigerate it, and drink it cold—tastes closer to Japanese konbini coffee than hot Starbucks.
