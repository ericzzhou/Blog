Why Buying Korean Skincare in the U.S. Requires Care

K-beauty's popularity has created a thriving grey market. Products shipped directly from Korea may be authentic but can arrive damaged, expired, or improperly stored (heat-sensitive serums and creams degrade in transit). Meanwhile, marketplace platforms have seen an increase in counterfeit versions of popular products.

Key risks to watch for:

  • Counterfeit products: Popular items like Beauty of Joseon sunscreen and Sulwhasoo serums are frequently counterfeited.
  • Expired stock: Some sellers discount near-expiry products without clear disclosure.
  • Heat damage: Serums, creams, and sunscreens can degrade if stored above 25°C during shipping.
  • Version mismatches: Korean domestic versions may differ from export versions in formulation.

How to Spot Counterfeit Korean Skincare

  • Packaging quality: Counterfeits often have slightly off colors, blurry text, or misspelled brand names.
  • Batch codes: Authentic products have batch/lot codes printed on the packaging. Verify with the brand if unsure.
  • Texture and scent: If the product smells or feels different from what you're used to, it may be fake.
  • Price: If Sulwhasoo serum is listed at $20 when the retail price is $60+, it's almost certainly counterfeit.
  • Seller verification: Buy from authorized retailers or official brand stores on marketplace platforms.
