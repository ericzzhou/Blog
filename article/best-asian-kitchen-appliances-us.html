01 · The Category

What are Asian kitchen appliances?

Asian kitchen appliances are specialized cooking devices designed for Asian cooking needs — most visibly rice cookers engineered for Japanese short-grain rice, which cook differently than generic Western models, and soy milk makers that are staples in Chinese households but nearly unknown in American kitchens. The category spans three groups: rice cookers with induction heating (IH) and fuzzy-logic settings for sushi, brown, porridge, and other rice; soy milk makers that produce fresh soy milk for about $0.50 per serving versus $3–5 store-bought; and multi-function cookers that combine 5–8 functions (soy milk, grain milk, soup, juice, baby food) in one device. Japanese and Taiwanese brands — Zojirushi, Tatung, and Joyoung are the most common in the U.S. — lead the category, and their appliances are typically built for 10+ year lifespans. For U.S. buyers, the main caveat is voltage: many Asian-market units run on 220V, so check compatibility before importing.

05 · Voltage Guide

Important: Voltage & Compatibility

When buying Asian appliances for U.S. use, pay attention to voltage:

  • 110V (U.S. standard): most Zojirushi and Tatung models sold through U.S. retailers are 110V. Check the product listing.
  • 100V (Japanese domestic): some Zojirushi models are 100V Japanese domestic — these need a step-down transformer (110V→100V) and may void warranty.
  • 220V (China/Taiwan domestic): avoid unless the listing explicitly states 110V/U.S. compatible.
Tip: buy from U.S.-based retailers (Yami, Amazon US) to ensure the correct voltage model with a U.S. warranty.
