Why Ube Is Becoming the Next Matcha

Published August 11, 2026 · Updated August 11, 2026

Matcha's rise followed a familiar path: an Asian staple → Instagram/TikTok visual → Starbucks menu → mainstream US grocery. In 2025–2026, ube started running the same playbook. Starbucks launched ube drinks in 2025 and expanded the lineup in spring 2026, and by mid-2026 trade press was already calling it "the next matcha."

What ube is

Ube is purple yam — a Filipino staple with a mild, nutty, vanilla-like sweetness and an unmistakable bright purple color. It's not the same as purple sweet potato (which is starchier and less sweet), though the two get mixed up constantly online. The visual is the hook: on camera, ube looks like nothing else on a menu.

The matcha pattern

  • Chain validation: Starbucks' 2025 Iced Ube Coconut Latte and Ube Espresso Martini gave the ingredient mainstream US exposure; its 2026 spring lineup (Ube Matcha Latte, Ube Vanilla Macchiato) extended it.
  • Social engine: purple drinks and pastries are built for short video — the same dynamic that pushed matcha (green) and taro (purple-ish) before it.
  • Supply catching up: ube powder, ube halaya (jam), and ube-flavored snacks are moving from Filipino grocery channels into general Asian e-commerce, the same way matcha powder did years ago.

What to buy in the US right now

Real ube (ube halaya, Filipino ube powder) is still scarce on mainstream Asian grocery platforms. The closest family that's widely available is purple sweet potato — same color story, similar snack appeal — plus matcha itself if you're already on the trend train.

If you want the ube-adjacent experience today:
  • Purple Sweet Potato Powder (150g) — mix into lattes, smoothies, and baking for the purple drink effect.
  • Purple Sweet Potato Bites (100g) — the chewy-snack version of the trend.
  • Already a matcha drinker? Marukyu Koyamaen matcha is the reference standard.

Note: as of August 2026, Yami doesn't stock genuine Filipino ube halaya/ube powder — we're not pretending purple sweet potato is ube. If you want the real thing, look for Philippine specialty grocers or wait for supply to catch up with the trend.

The bottom line

Ube is following matcha's exact playbook, and the ingredient is one to watch for the next 12–18 months. For now, the practical US takeaway is simple: the trend is real, genuine ube products are still catching up, and purple sweet potato is the closest thing you can buy today.
